Crew: 2 mistri + 3 mazdoor, living on site. This is the right size for this job. Add extra daily mazdoors only on digging, PCC, slab-pour and floor days, and a 3rd mistri only for plaster week. With 3–4 mistri and 3–4 mazdoor the brickwork finishes a few days sooner, but every curing wait leaves 7–8 people sitting, and wages go over Rs 5 lakh. Every wait is filled with work that does not depend on it: boundary footings during DPC curing, the front wall while lintels set and while the steel is tied, and the left wall, plaster and parapet during slab curing. Only 6 days in slab curing have nothing left to do, so the crew goes home (chutti) and 1 mazdoor waters the roof.

Week 1: marking, digging, PCC in every trench

Days 1–5 · 3 mistri days · 28 mazdoor days · Rs 57,900 wages

  1. Day1

    Demarcation (nishan dehi) Check

    Head mistri marks all wall centre lines with rope and chuna, including the left and front boundary walls. Check every room corner is square (equal diagonals). Set a fixed level mark from road level.

    Mistri × 1Mazdoor × 2Engineer visit

    On site: Chuna, rope, pegs, tape. Round 1 material starts arriving.

    What to inspect
    • Each room's two diagonals are equal (within ½″), so corners are square
    • Wall centre lines match the drawing sizes
    • A fixed level mark (benchmark) is set for all heights
    • Left passage marked: 3′ path + 2′ soil strip
  2. Day2

    Digging (khudai)

    Dig ALL trenches now, house and boundary together: 2′-6″ × 3′-6″ house walls, 2′ × 2′-6″ parda walls, 1′-6″ × 2′ left boundary, 2′ × 2′-6″ front boundary, plus the 6 future stubs. 3 crew mazdoors + 4 extra daily mazdoors.

    Mazdoor × 7

    On site: Shovels, tasla. Keep dug mitti on the plot.

    What to inspect
    • Trench widths: 2′-6″ house walls, 2′-0″ parda, 1′-6″ left boundary, 2′ front
    • Depth 3′-6″ everywhere, bottom flat and level
    • Sides straight, no loose soil falling in
    • No water standing in the trench
    • Plan B: the 6 stubs (2′ each) for future rooms are dug too
  3. Day3

    Digging

    Continue trenches.

    Mazdoor × 7
    What to inspect
    • Trench widths: 2′-6″ house walls, 2′-0″ parda, 1′-6″ left boundary, 2′ front
    • Depth 3′-6″ everywhere, bottom flat and level
    • Sides straight, no loose soil falling in
    • No water standing in the trench
    • Plan B: the 6 stubs (2′ each) for future rooms are dug too
  4. Day4

    Finish digging + trench bottom

    Finish digging. Water and ram trench bottoms. Spray anti-termite. Lay 3″ Ravi rait bed.

    Mazdoor × 6

    On site: Anti-termite, ~200 cft Ravi rait

    What to inspect
    • Trench widths: 2′-6″ house walls, 2′-0″ parda, 1′-6″ left boundary, 2′ front
    • Depth 3′-6″ everywhere, bottom flat and level
    • Sides straight, no loose soil falling in
    • No water standing in the trench
    • Plan B: the 6 stubs (2′ each) for future rooms are dug too
    • Bottom watered and rammed hard
    • Anti-termite sprayed on bottom and sides
    • Rait bed 3″ thick, even and wet
  5. Day5

    PCC in every trench Check

    Mistris arrive. 6″ PCC 1:4:8 in all trenches in one day: house, boundary walls and the 6 future stubs. 3 crew mazdoors + 3 extra.

    Mistri × 2Mazdoor × 6Mixer

    On site: ~38 bags cement, ~191 cft Chenab rait, ~383 cft bajri

    What to inspect
    • Mix 1:4:8 by tasla count (1 cement : 4 rait : 8 bajri)
    • Thickness 6″ (check with a marked stick)
    • PCC covers the full trench width
    • Top is level
    • Kept wet after pouring
    • Plan B: PCC poured in the 6 future stubs
